PLANT PHYSIOLOGY AND ENERGY

Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy. Chloroplasts in leaf cells contain chlorophyll, which absorbs mainly red and blue wavelengths. The light reactions split water and release oxygen as a by-product.

The Calvin cycle uses ATP and NADPH from the light reactions to fix carbon dioxide. Rubisco catalyzes the first stable carbon fixation step. Most terrestrial carbon enters the biosphere through this pathway.

Photosynthetic rate depends on light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration, and temperature. At high light levels, electron transport can exceed the capacity of carbon fixation. Drought closes stomata and limits carbon uptake.

Water moves through plants in the xylem. Transpiration pulls water from the roots to the leaves under tension. Cohesion between water molecules keeps the column intact.

Root pressure can push water upward when transpiration is low. Aquaporins regulate water movement across cell membranes. The Casparian strip forces water through living root cells.

Stomata balance carbon gain against water loss. Guard cells change shape as potassium ions enter or leave. Abscisic acid signals stomatal closure during drought stress.

Growth begins with cell division in meristems. Apical meristems extend roots and shoots, while lateral meristems thicken stems. Hormones coordinate division, elongation, and differentiation.

Auxin redistributes in response to light and gravity. Cytokinins promote cell division, and gibberellins promote stem elongation. Ethylene controls fruit ripening and leaf abscission.

Environmental signals shape growth. Photoperiod controls flowering in many species. Vernalization exposes some plants to cold before flowering. Resource allocation shifts toward roots when nutrients are scarce.
